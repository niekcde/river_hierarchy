Example 48: reach 36 and outlet input sensitivity

SCOPE
Input-only diagnostic. Original supplied-type local-inertial kernel with critical-flow cap retained; no positivity limiter, no q-centred smoothing. Fixed n=.020799605931143154, forcing, topology and reach lengths. Each variant re-equilibrated under its own geometry, then run through the same event. Original data and production settings were not edited.

CHANGES TESTED
W: restore reach 36 wet measured width, 21.319629 -> 158.592645 m. This tests removing the flagged correction; it does not assert the measurement is hydraulically correct.
R: halve reach 36 head drop while preserving its midpoint elevation. Node 24 falls by .11646 m and node 25 rises by .11646 m; slope 3.813649 -> 1.906824 m/km. Both node values are shared by every incident reach, so slopes of reaches 33,35,36,37,38 change consistently. This is an explicitly artificial sensitivity, not an observation correction.
O: raise outlet node 126 from 1733.961265 to raw matched WSE 1737.680160 m. Keep upstream node 125 fixed. Terminal reach-188 slope becomes 1.996035 instead of 2.352452 m/km. The normal-flow outlet closure remains in place and uses the updated terminal slope. This tests the geometry AND its consistent normal-flow rating, not a different boundary-condition type.
All eight W/R/O combinations were tested at 100 and 50 m. Two additional cases use raw WSE at nodes 24/25: 1916.758400 and 1915.692170 m; reach-36 slope then becomes 8.728621 m/km (steeper, not flatter). Raw36 alone and W+raw36+O were run at both grids. W+O and W+R+O were also repeated at 50 m, CFL .175. Total 22 completed event simulations, each with steady-state preparation.

CHECKS
All variant widths/slopes remain positive; connected reaches retain identical node heads. Actual solver inputs are saved as event_input.npz; copied physical_parameters.parquet includes historical MC metadata that is not used by the dynamic solver. Manning n in the solver and result metadata is authoritative. Every steady state met max cell flow imbalance / inlet flow <1e-8. Spinup clipping is recorded separately and is not silently treated as validation.

INTERPRETATION
Every 100 m event remains free of negative-depth clipping. Every tested 50 m event still clips negative depths, including combined changes and smaller-CFL controls. These 50 m hydrographs are invalid predictions: clipping creates water. Differences in spike size or clipping volume are not treated as a ranking of input plausibility; the unstable trajectories can depend on very small initial-state differences.

The restored width improves local conditions at reach 36; the adjusted outlet reduces the terminal slope and changes depth there. These local improvements do not eliminate the broader unstable regime. This supports the prior straight-channel benchmark evidence that the problem is not solely a bottleneck at reach 36 or this particular outlet elevation adjustment.
It does NOT establish that the original width correction or outlet match is correct, or that no conceivable geometry/boundary alternative could help. No independent remapping or elevation survey was performed. The raw WSE alternative is not a verified bed elevation.

DECISION
None of these input variants is promoted into the production setup. Review the flagged width and outlet match on their own observational merits; do not tune them to suppress instability. The tested corrections are insufficient to make the current local-inertial formulation reliable at 50 m under the fixed roughness and forcing. A 25 m follow-up was not warranted because no 50 m variant passed the basic no-clipping requirement.
