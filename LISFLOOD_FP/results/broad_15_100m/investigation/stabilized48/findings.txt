Example 48: documented q-centred stabilization — diagnostic experiment

Outcome: substantially improved 50 m behaviour, but no demonstrated grid convergence. Do not promote these runs into the calibrated broad-study results.

METHOD AND SCOPE
Manning n remains 0.020799605931143154. Measured widths, bed proxies, forcing, physical topology and normal-flow outlet remain fixed. Theta is a numerical weighting, not a calibrated physical parameter. Each case is re-equilibrated before the event. Original-network cases only; no collapses in this focused solver test.
The momentum numerator uses qbar = theta*q[j] + (1-theta)/2*(q[left]+q[right]), evaluated from the OLD face flows simultaneously. Friction uses the original local old q, not qbar. Pressure gradient, critical cap and conservative donor-volume limiter remain unchanged.
The published regular-grid q-centred formulation is applied only where three consecutive faces lie within the same physical reach with equal widths and face distances. Physical reach boundaries/junctions use theta=1. This is our explicit network adaptation; it is not the official LISFLOOD-FP executable, nor a validated general junction extension. Smoothed faces: 941/1522 at 100 m, 2272/2887 at 50 m, 4998/5623 at 25 m.
Primary source: de Almeida et al. (2012), https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2011WR011570
Equation independently checked against the published form reproduced in Deltares Wflow documentation (2D overland section, NOT its 1D river scheme): https://deltares.github.io/Wflow.jl/v0.7.3/model_docs/lateral/local-inertial/

VERIFICATION
Theta=1 gives bit-for-bit identical outputs to the conservative baseline. One q-centred step matches independent vectorized arithmetic. Uniform Manning equilibrium and lake-at-rest checks pass. 72 periodic uniform-channel mode tests match independently derived linear growth factors within 0.1% (actual differences much smaller). These checks establish implementation consistency, not full hydraulic accuracy.

For 50 m theta=.7, halving CFL changes peak selection by 40 min but changes the common-window centroid by only -1.270 seconds. Thus the bulk timing is much less sensitive than the crest maximum. Smaller ripples/crest reshaping still prevent treating peak calibration as robust.
The nominal matched-diffusion control (theta=.8, CFL=.35 versus theta=.9, CFL=.175) gives the same +207.5 min selected-peak error and similar heights (~318 m3/s). This supports sensitivity to numerical damping, rather than timestep accuracy alone. Holding theta fixed while reducing dt increases smoothing per simulated second. The matching is nominal, not exact, since the friction denominator also depends on dt.

UNIFORM CHANNEL MECHANISM
For the uniform analogue of reach 161, at 50 m and dt=3 s, one-hour amplitude factor falls from 1.563 (theta=1) to 1.314 (theta=.7), but remains above 1. Growth is reduced, not eliminated. This test contains no junctions, outlet, wetting/drying or cap activity. It demonstrates that unresolved growing modes are not exclusively a junction treatment problem. It does not uniquely partition the complete network error.

DECISION
Do not choose theta by matching the downstream gauge, and do not recalibrate this variant yet. Retain the original production code and mark these results experimental. A defensible next comparison is an independent full shallow-water reference on straight-channel benchmarks with the same h, slope and roughness, followed by a junction benchmark. Additional diffusion may suppress a symptom without establishing that the simplified equations are appropriate for these hydraulics. No full shallow-water solver benchmark was run in this test.
