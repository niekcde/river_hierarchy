With it retained, example 48 at n=0.0207996059 has a 100 m timing error of -0.5 min; halving CFL gives -0.5 min. Both event runs conserve volume without negative-depth clipping. Hydraulic evidence: current-bed steady-storage and reach-flow diagnostics are in 37_current_bed_storage_comparison.csv and 37_current_bed_flow_diagnosis.csv. The larger collapse adds about 3.08 million m3 of local-model storage versus about 0.063 million m3 in a normal-depth calculation retaining fixed MC allocation at the same n and initial inflow. Local flow allocation differs from fixed MC shares; some branches reverse. This supports a storage/redistribution mechanism, but is not a unique attribution to backwater or proof of real-river behaviour. Storage divided by inflow is not asserted to equal flood-wave travel time.

Combined junction diagnostic: four full min-width connections at a symmetric 2-in/2-out node overcount connecting width. Partitioning interface widths restores the 2 m uniform benchmark to 2.22e-15 m error. In the isolated example-37 legacy-bed test at frozen n, changing only those interfaces alters the paired collapse timing effects by at most 0.129 seconds. This does not explain the large delay; the alternative is a sensitivity hypothesis, not an adopted production fix.

Limiter diagnosis: legacy-roughness failures in 15, 22 and 48 activate the cap in steady initial flow before the flood. The capped interfaces are not combined merge/split nodes. At the frozen capped profile, the uncapped stationary momentum balance would demand Fr about 1.09 (15), up to 3.77 (22), and up to 1.10 (48). These are diagnostic demands at that profile, not predictions of an uncapped solution. Smaller timesteps do not remove the steady cap. Increasing n can move a case into the uncapped regime, but does not by itself validate the hydraulics. The limiter is part of the supplied scheme; our strict acceptance rule deliberately excludes active limiting.
