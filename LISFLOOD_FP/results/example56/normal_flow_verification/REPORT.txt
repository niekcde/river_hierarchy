FINDINGS
32 hydraulic network runs and 10 MC runs completed, plus eight initial verification checks and independent steady-backwater/pulse refinement benchmarks.
The unsegmented and 200m-floor configurations produce approximately +1.8 minute collapse peak delays. Refinement instead converges toward an advance: -17.04 min at max250m, -17.38 min at max125m, -17.58 min at max62.5m. The saved MC reference gives -36.83 min.
The 125m CFL0.7 cases are INVALID: artificial clipping water was approximately 1,047,900m3 original and 33,530m3 collapse. A smooth peak did not reveal this problem; full-series outlet changes reached 31.08 and 10.30m3/s relative to valid half-step runs. CFL0.35 eliminates clipping; further halving makes changes below 0.1 second to peak timing at this grid. The timestep coefficient is not universally safe on this network discretization.
Valid selected normal-flow runs have depths near 2.60m; refined maximum face Froude is about 0.38. Valid event runs conserve volume with relative residual below 3.5e-13 and have no clipping or critical-flow limiting.
The 125m-to-62.5m collapse peak/centroid changes are 0.199/0.242 minutes. This supports a working 125m grid with CFL0.35 for example56, while retaining 62.5m as a reference. This is a study-specific tolerance, not proof of accuracy on other networks.
Outlet slope half/double and a separate fixed-stage sensitivity alter absolute depths and timing, but collapse peak advances remain between 16.82 and 17.26 minutes at max250m. These boundary choices do not explain the sign change between coarse and fine grids.
Independent steady local-inertial backwater ODE errors decrease from 2.84mm to 0.38mm under refinement. This verifies a smooth-channel equilibrium, not every network junction configuration.
MC further subdivision changes the collapse peak advance from 36.830 to 36.880 minutes; centroid change remains effectively 36.860 minutes. Thus the remaining MC/local-inertial discrepancy is not removed by the tested numerical refinements.
MATLAB agreement is still untested. No calibration was performed.

INTERPRETATION LIMITS
Lake-at-rest, steady Manning and conservation tests verify specific properties, not the full network junction approximation. Pairwise connections and minimum interface widths are inherited from the supplied code. No independently verified network hydraulic solution is available. Spatial convergence within this formulation does not establish field accuracy. Bed elevations remain agreed proxies.
Reduced water-surface slopes relative to bed slopes identify backwater-like profiles, not proof that backwater causes the MC timing difference. Inertia, changing depth, branch fractions, outlet closure and cross-section friction conventions all differ.
