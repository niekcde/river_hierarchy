Timestep interpretation
Let denom=dt+2K(1-X), a=(dt-2KX)/denom, b=(dt+2KX)/denom, d=(2K(1-X)-dt)/denom.
Current fixed-X Muskingum practice drops dt>=2KX (a>=0), retains dt<=2K(1-X) (d>=0), and audits finite/nonnegative flows. Dropping that lower bound is not equivalent to dropping all accuracy requirements.
For negative Cunge X, a is automatically positive but b may be negative. If ALL three coefficients were required nonnegative, the interval would be 2K*abs(X)<=dt<=2K(1-X). The globally required interval is absent in this test. Negative b need not produce a negative impulse response: h0=a and hj=(b+d*a)*d^(j-1), with b+d*a=4K*dt/denom^2>0. Thus a>=0 and 0<=d<1 give a nonnegative response even if b<0. The tested adaptive rule guarantees a>=0; selecting dt below the common upper bound gives d>=0. Allowance for signed previous-inflow coefficients must be explicit in Cunge preparation/validation; the current fixed-X policy name alone is insufficient.

With Cunge K=dx/c and X=.5*(1-Ld/dx), the retained upper bound becomes dt<=(dx+Ld)/c. This is a non-oscillatory previous-outflow-coefficient guard, not a general necessary condition for linear stability. For fixed coefficients K>0 and X<=.5 on our feed-forward network with conservative nonnegative split fractions, linear boundedness/non-amplification does not require every routing coefficient to be nonnegative. Stable output can nevertheless be inaccurate or undershoot.

The strictest upper limit over these 80 states is 48.3797 s for current floored preparation and 15.65984 s for actual-length and adaptive cases. dt=15 s respects the retained upper guard; actual implementation should derive a common clock from all states with its safety margin and forcing cadence, rather than hardcode 15 s. Recalculate bounds after calibration or any hydraulic change. Lowering dt does not make a negative current-inflow coefficient positive; for positive X it can make that coefficient more negative.

Recommendation
Use actual lengths and remove the blanket 200 m minimum-length modification as the preferred candidate, with a newly selected common clock. Replace blanket subdivision with tested selective subdivision rather than remove subdivision everywhere. The L/Ld rule is a concrete inexpensive positivity-preserving candidate. Preserve grids on inherited unchanged reaches between parents and children under the agreed reference policy. Verify accuracy over the full intended population and recalibrate after changing the routing configuration.

Caveats
These results are for Cunge X recomputed at each spatial resolution. They must not be transferred directly to fixed-X Muskingum: its nominal diffusivity c*dx*(.5-X) changes with dx. Cunge matches nominal diffusivity across resolutions, but not every higher-order property of the diffusion equation. In these tests substantial absolute peak-time discrepancies from the independent diffusion benchmark remain even between the 100 and 200 m resolutions. Agreement between those grids, positivity, and consistent pair signs therefore do not by themselves establish physical validation.
